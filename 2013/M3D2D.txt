Unlimited Detail?

The idea is to scatter points among view subpyramids corresponding to pixels. Now there can be many points in a view subpyramid & we want at most one: a frontmost one.
A solution is to view this as a "mass connected process" i.e., to analyze both the object & the view. The object is an octree & the view is a quadtree. Implicit are the view pyramid & its subpyramids (subspaces of the quadtree).
Note that the quadtree corresponds to 3-D subspaces of the view, not merely 2-D subspaces of the view plane.
Example: imagine a pyramid & a cube.
If the pyramid has no quadrant then the color of the corresponding pixel = the color of the cube & this pyramid is labeled as BLACK.
Otherwise the pyramid has quadrants: if the cube is in one of them then apply this procedure to the cube & to that quadrant/subpyramid.
Otherwise consider the cube's octants/subcubes in front-to-back order. If a subcube isn't empty & has no front faces (the observer is in that subcube) or there is a
front face that isn't occluded then apply this procedure to that subcube & to the pyramid.
Before returning, update the label of the pyramid based on its quadrants' labels i.e., BLACK or (inclusive) WHITE.

How does one decide whether a front face isn't occluded? The idea is to find a subsquare of the face that is in a WHITE subpyramid:
if the given square is in a WHITE pyramid then the corresponding cube isn't occluded. Otherwise if the square is in a pyramid's quadrant that isn't BLACK then apply this procedure to the square & to that subpyramid.
Otherwise (i.e., the square spans many quadrants) apply this procedure to the quadrants/subsquares of the square & to the given pyramid.

Note that descending the octrees in the pyramids in front-to-back order gives frontmost points at the bottom of the quadtree of pyramids. Also note that we shall not subdivide a cube or square
if, for example, 2 ^ s - 1 <= 2 * z / 2 ^ r (for a 90° fov.) where s = log(cube/square size), z is the view space z of the LUF vertex (or center) of the cube/square & r = log(vres.).
Finally note that, in view space, you need mostly comparisons for the sorting of cubes or squares among the view subspaces (extend I3D2D's idea below).

/* O object octree, Q view quadtree of planes inducing the view pyramid & its subpyramids.
    M3D2D isn't called if O is occluded, therefore O != 0 (and is in no BLACK subtree of Q, Q inc.). For argument's sake, the quadtree is full & has height log(vres.) + 3.
    The quadtree paths "0...", "1...", "2...", "31...", "32..." & "33..." are BLACK while all "30..." are WHITE. A consequence is view pyramid culling.
    label(Q) is 2-bit: one for WHITE & one for BLACK (BLACK | WHITE = GRAY).
    front_faces(Oi, obs.) is the set of the front faces of octant Oi with respect to obs., the observer's octant rel. to O. Note that front_faces(O, obs.) is a subset of front_faces(Oi, obs.) & that Oi ^ (XOR) obs. gives
    the dirs. of the remaining front faces. Example: Oi ^ obs = 101 means that there's a front face perpendicular to the x axis (0th bit = 1) & a ff. perp. to z (2nd bit = 1). There are 2 faces per dir., the front faces
    are given by the 0th & 2nd bits of obs. (if 0 then left/front, otherwise right/back), union the ffs. of the father.
    The analysis of the octree by the quadtree of quadrisecting pairs of planes is carried through in view space.
    Except the expressions of the object space basis & origin in view space, no * (or / or float) need be feared (cube or square in subpyramid = comparisons in view space). */

M3D2D(O, Q) /* mass 3-D to 2-D conversion */
{
    if (Q = (0)) {  /* leaf Q (has only empty subtrees) */
        color(Q) = color(O)
        for (0 <= i < 4)
            label(Qi) = BLACK
    } else {
        if (O in Qi)    /* When O is so far that its edges' size fits in a leaf subpyramid (corresponding to a pixel), O = its LUF vertex: no more subdivisions than demanded by the visual (the same holds for the squares below) */
            M3D2D(O, Qi)    /* label(Qi) != BLACK because otherwise O would be occluded & M3D2D would not have been called */
        else
            for (i in front_to_back(O, obs.))
                if (Oi & (!front_faces(Oi, obs.) or j in front_faces(Oi, obs.) & V(j, Q)))  /* a sufficient rejection condition: octant Oi is an empty octree or has front faces, all of which are occluded */
                    M3D2D(Oi, Q)
    }
    label(Q) = label(Q0) | label(Q1) | label(Q2) | label(Q3)    /* redundant when O did not contribute. But we're presenting an idea here */
}

/* The (V)isible predicate is as follows (j is a square, Q a quadtree, j0, j1 etc. are the LU, RU etc. subsquares of j, Qi is the ith subtree of Q) */

V(j, Q) = label(Q) = WHITE or j in Qi & label(Qi) != BLACK & V(j, Qi) or !j in Qi & (V(j0, Q) or V(j1, Q) or V(j2, Q) or V(j3, Q))

But how can one perform a perspective "individual 3-D to 2-D conversion" (B. Dell) that is free from floats, * or /? See the below C pseudocode (C's precedence & order of evaluation hold).

/* x, y, z are the view space coordinates of the point, in the half-open view pyramid, to be projected
    L = U = -z, R = D = z (for a 90° fov.), X = Y = 0 & s = log(vres.) */

I3D2D(x, y, z, L, U, R, D, X, Y, s) /* individual 3-D to 2-D conversion */
{
    if (s)
        return I3D2D(x, y, z, -(x < L + R >> 1) & L | -(L + R >> 1 <= x) & L + R >> 1, -(y < U + D >> 1) & U | -(U + D >> 1 <= y) & U + D >> 1, -(x < L + R >> 1) & L + R >> 1 | -(L + R >> 1 <= x) & R, -(y < U + D >> 1) & U + D >> 1 | -(U + D >> 1 <= y) & D, X + (-(x < L + R >> 1) ^ 1 << s - 2 + 1) + (x < L + R >> 1), Y + (-(y < U + D >> 1) ^ 1 << s - 2 + 1) + (y < U + D >> 1), s - 1)
    return (X >> 1, Y >> 1)
}

Note that this procedure can be extended into a general texture mapper that subdivides the polygons & that is free from floats, * or /: another example of "mass 3-D to 2-D conversion".
Mass 3-D to 2-D conversions (e.g., texture mapper or octree splatter) associate to each vertex of the mass (e.g., polygon or cube) a frame L, U, R, D (left, up, right, down) i.e., a pyramid's transverse section, the plane of which contains the vertex. Against the midpoints of these frames are the vertices compared.
L, U, R or D of midpoint = midpoints of L, U, R or Ds resp. (Cf. https://docs.google.com/file/d/0B0Tw1fnDScRsQkk1eklNeXJyMDg/edit?usp=sharing).

Very relevant: https://docs.google.com/file/d/0B0Tw1fnDScRsTnM5T1dPWjlsZWs/edit?usp=sharing (cf. "PRIP_Meagher.pdf" & "US4694404.pdf" in regard to UD).

How are the frames of the children (whether cubes or squares) derived?
Suppose the frames Lj, Uj, Rj & Dj (0 <= j < 8) for the father's vertices. We would like to know the frame Lij, Uij, Rij & Dij for the vertex j of the ith child.
Xij = (Xi + Xj) / 2 where X = L, U, R or D.
